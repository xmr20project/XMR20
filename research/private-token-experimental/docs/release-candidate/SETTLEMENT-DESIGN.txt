SETTLEMENT DESIGN — THE TWO COUNTEREXAMPLES, WHICH ASSUMPTION FAILS, WHAT COULD CLOSE THEM
research/privacy-release-candidate-20261025, 25 October 2026. Internal analysis, not an audit. Nothing here is a fix.
Bridge payouts and native XMR trading remain GATED. Private transfers are unaffected by this document.

0. GROUND RULES
  - Monero has no finality. There is no maximum reorganisation depth (18-block reorg on record, Sept 2025). Any construction
    that says "after N confirmations" is an ASSUMPTION about depth, never a guarantee.
  - The private format exists to avoid the native source-output -> key-image association (review/03). No construction below
    reintroduces it: records never name a real ring member, a key image or an amount of the carrier.
  - The Monero node validates ONLY the native transaction. It never validates a token record. Whatever a token record says,
    the XMR in the same transaction moves if the native transaction is valid.
  - "More confirmations", "two nodes agree", "wallet reservations", "rate limits" change WHEN or HOW OFTEN the loss happens.
    None of them eliminates either counterexample. They are controls, not solutions.

1. COUNTEREXAMPLE A — BRIDGE: PAYOUT ON A BURN THAT LATER DISAPPEARS
  Reproduced live in this candidate: settlement/bridge-rollback-loss.test.mjs -> settlement/evidence/bridge-payout-then-
  rollback-live-<date>.json (PASS = loss reproduced). Prior evidence preserved: settlement/evidence/bridge-payout-then-
  rollback-20261022-verified-oSuMwR.json (privacy-integration-20261022).
  Sequence: deposit 100 X -> credit -> A burns 40 X (XB3 redemption, 10 confirmations) -> operator pays 40 X (net of fee) on
  Solana (irreversible program execution) -> rollback of 11 blocks removes the burn -> a second installation of the same
  phrase (backup; no journal reservation) spends the same 40 X note to B -> B holds 40 X AND the Solana payout stands.
  Operator backing: reserve = owed before; after, owed exceeds reserve by exactly 40,000,000; incident paidWithoutBurn; halt.
  THE ASSUMPTION THAT FAILS: "a burn with CONFIRMATIONS confirmations is final, so an irreversible action on another chain may
  be taken against it". Two independent facts break it together: (i) Monero can drop the burn at any depth; (ii) once it is
  dropped the burned note is simply unspent, and NOTHING in the protocol remembers that it was once burned — a burn is not a
  nullifier in the Solana world. The local journal reservation is per installation; a backup, a second installation or a
  fresh recovery from the phrase does not have it. Preventing backups is wallet convenience, not security.
  WHO LOSES: the operator's backing shared by all holders (no per-holder insurance), by exactly the paid amount per event.
  Exposure controls bound the RATE (payouts per window), not the loss (ERRATUM: 400 exposed under a 200 cap).

2. COUNTEREXAMPLE B — NATIVE TRADING: BUYER PAYS XMR, RECEIVES NO TOKENS
  Preserved evidence: settlement/evidence/trading-buyer-pays-no-tokens-20261022-verified-nejEGH.json (real-daemon rollback,
  privacy-settlement-policy-20261022); abstract model re-run here: settlement/model/finality-model.test.mjs 9/9 (the model
  PASSES by retaining the loss case: "native child ancestry alone cannot require valid parent token data").
  Sequence: seller publishes a RESERVATION record locking the lot (10 confirmations) -> buyer's signing controller releases a
  native purchase transaction (XMR to the seller, buyer's receipt record in its tx_extra, bound to the reservation) -> rollback
  removes the reservation -> a genuine conflicting spend of the seller's note (by the seller's keys: another installation or a
  stale backup, or a dishonest seller) becomes canonical -> the captured purchase is rebroadcast and MINES: the native
  transaction is valid, the receipt record is rejected by every verifier -> seller has XMR, buyer has nothing.
  THE ASSUMPTION THAT FAILS: "the canonical prefix through the reservation remains unchanged for the lifetime of the exported
  native signature" (conditional finality) AND "a valid native transaction implies a valid token outcome". The second is
  false by construction: the node does not read records. Once the buyer's signature exists it is a bearer instrument on XMR
  only. Honest wallets can halt; nobody can revoke a captured Monero signature.
  WHO LOSES: the buyer, the full price plus fee; the seller (or whoever holds the seller's keys) gains.

3. WHAT A REAL SOLUTION WOULD HAVE TO DO, AND WHY THE NATIVE LAYER CANNOT
  To eliminate B the XMR payment must become INVALID when the token outcome is invalid. Monero transactions have no scripts,
  no covenants, no timelocks after inclusion and no way to reference token state. The only lever that makes a native spend
  depend on something is the KEY: who can produce the signature. The only lever that ties two token events together is the
  RECORD: the same record cannot be valid twice. Every construction must be built from those two levers.
  To eliminate A the Solana payout must become REVERSIBLE for as long as the burn can disappear, or the burn must be made
  unforgettable. A burn cannot be made unforgettable on Monero (the chain forgets it with the block). It can be made
  unforgettable on Solana only if Solana knows the burn — i.e. if the payout itself records the burn's note commitment and
  the credit path refuses to re-credit a note whose burn was paid. That turns a re-spend after rollback into a loss for the
  RE-SPENDER's counterparty (B, who accepted a note whose burn was already paid) instead of the operator — it moves the loss,
  it does not remove it, and B cannot know (privacy) that the note was once burned. Rejected: it silently changes who pays.

4. CANDIDATE CONSTRUCTIONS EVALUATED (none demonstrated; none adopted)
  4.1 PROTOCOL: bundled trade transaction (one native tx carries the buyer's XMR payment AND the seller's pre-signed transfer
      record to the buyer). Reorg-consistent by construction: both legs are in one transaction, so a rollback reverts both
      together; no key image or source output is named. RESIDUAL: the native tx can still mine while the record is invalid
      if the seller's note was spent elsewhere in a transaction that became canonical first (seller equivocation, including
      an honest seller's stale backup). So B is narrowed from "any reorg across the reservation" to "seller double-signed the
      same note", which is attributable to the seller's keys but not provable to a third party without the seller's view
      key. Custody: none. Privacy: unchanged (ordinary carrier). Liveness: buyer needs the seller's signed record off-chain
      first; seller needs nothing. Loss: buyer, on seller equivocation. CLASS: protocol improvement, not a solution.
  4.2 PROTOCOL: adaptor signature on the buyer's payment, completed by a secret the seller must reveal to publish the record.
      Gives atomicity at PUBLICATION, not at finality: after the record is published the seller holds a complete signature and
      can broadcast it after a rollback that removed the record. Same residual as 4.1, more complexity, and Monero's CLSAG
      adaptor machinery is research-grade. CLASS: no improvement over 4.1 for this failure. Not pursued.
  4.3 ESCROW (two-party): buyer pays into a 2-of-2 shared-key output (additive key shares, Monero native multisig or a shared
      one-time key); release transaction co-signed only after the buyer sees the receipt record at the agreed depth; refund
      co-signed otherwise. No timelock exists on Monero, so a party that disappears locks the funds forever. Safety: no
      asymmetric theft; Liveness: fails on any non-cooperation; Custody: joint; Privacy: the release/refund are ordinary
      transactions. CLASS: trusted-cooperation arrangement; converts loss into stuck funds; not acceptable without an arbiter.
  4.4 ESCROW (operator arbiter, 2-of-3): as 4.3 plus an operator share that can complete release or refund after an evidence
      window. CLASS: TRUSTED OPERATOR with custody over trade funds during the window; the operator learns trade identities.
      It removes the asymmetric loss at the price of trust and custody. Honest option if the product accepts it.
  4.5 SELLER BOND: seller posts XMR collateral with the operator; buyer claims it on proven non-delivery. Proof of non-delivery
      requires the seller's view key or the operator reading the receipt outcome — the operator decides; CLASS: trusted
      operator + compensation arrangement; privacy cost on the seller.
  4.6 CONFIRMATION POLICY + RESERVE (bridge): payout only at depth D with a funded loss reserve sized to an explicit assumed
      depth; incident halt on any accepted-prefix change. CLASS: PRODUCT decision with a stated assumption. Loss bound only
      under the assumption; unbounded if the assumption fails. This is what the lab operator already does (D=10, lab reserve).
  4.7 REVERSIBLE PAYOUT ESCROW (bridge): the Solana payout lands in a program escrow with a clawback window T; the operator
      (or a 2-of-3 with the redeemer) can revoke within T if the burn left canonical history; after T the payout is final.
      Loss then requires a Monero reorganisation deeper than D that also happens AFTER T has elapsed; D and T are explicit
      numbers. CLASS: trusted operator (clawback authority) + confirmation policy; it does not reintroduce any disclosure;
      it changes the exposure from "any reorg > D" to "reorg > D after T", which is a product-grade improvement but still an
      assumption. Requires a new Solana program path (not present in the compiled custody used here).
  4.8 KEEP TRADING OFF THE PRIVATE FORMAT: trade only public tokens or trade through the bridge + a Solana venue. CLASS:
      product decision; no settlement risk on the private format; the native marketplace stays off.

5. RECOMMENDATION (for the product owner; nothing adopted by this candidate)
  Bridge (A): adopt 4.7 + 4.6 together as the design to review: explicit depth D, clawback window T, funded reserve sized to
  the assumed depth, incident halt, two independent nodes gating payout admission (implemented here as a gate, section 7 of
  RELEASE-CHECKLIST). State plainly that loss is possible for reorganisations deeper than D after T, and that the operator holds
  clawback authority during T. Until the Solana escrow path exists and is reviewed, payouts stay gated.
  Trading (B): no protocol construction demonstrated. 4.1 is the right protocol primitive to build next (it is strictly better
  than the current reservation/purchase pair and needs no new disclosure), but it does not remove seller-equivocation loss; the
  remaining choice is 4.4/4.5 (trusted operator, custody, identity exposure to the operator) or 4.8 (keep native trading off
  the private format). Native trading therefore stays GATED. Do not describe 4.1 as atomic.
  What this candidate implements on the basis of this analysis: nothing that claims to close A or B. It implements the gate
  (independent node agreement for payout admission) and keeps every verdict NO-GO for bridging and trading.

6. CONTROLS THAT MUST NOT BE PRESENTED AS SOLUTIONS
  more confirmations (changes D, not the existence of the loss); two nodes agreeing (protects against one lying/eclipsed node,
  not against a real reorganisation both nodes follow); wallet reservations (per installation; backups and fresh recoveries do
  not have them); rate limits (bound payouts per window; a single rollback can remove several windows' burns at once).
