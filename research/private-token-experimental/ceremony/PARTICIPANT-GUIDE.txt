XMR20 PRIVATE-TOKEN KEY CEREMONY — PARTICIPANT GUIDE (DRAFT, 25 October 2026)

Status: this is a draft for the owner to review. Nobody has been invited yet. The ceremony has not been scheduled.
The software it describes has been rehearsed end to end on one laptop (see ceremony/README.txt), but the code has not
had an independent security review. Do not circulate this guide until that review has happened and the owner has
fixed the tool version and circuit fingerprints below.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
XMR20 private tokens are protected by zero-knowledge proofs. Proofs need a set of public "parameter" files that every
wallet and indexer uses. Creating those files produces a secret by-product. Anyone who kept that secret could forge
tokens forever and nobody could tell. To make sure the secret is gone, the files are created by a chain of people:
each person mixes in their own random secret and then destroys it. The result is safe as long as at least ONE person
in the chain was honest. You are being asked to be one of those people. The whole job takes well under an hour and
you never touch money, keys, servers or anyone's wallet.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT YOU NEED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - A computer you control (laptop or desktop; macOS or Linux; about 2 GB free disk, 8 GB RAM or more).
  - Rust installed (https://rustup.rs). You will build the small tool yourself from source. Please do NOT use a
    binary sent to you by the coordinator; the point is that you do not have to trust us.
  - About 40 minutes. On a 2024 laptop one contribution takes roughly 15 minutes for the "normal" circuit and
    7 minutes for the "boundary" circuit. Slower machines take longer; that is fine.
  - A place to post a short hash that we cannot edit: your own X/Twitter, GitHub, Mastodon, Nostr or similar.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. BEFORE YOU START — CHECK YOU HAVE THE RIGHT CEREMONY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The coordinator will have PUBLISHED these before the first contribution. Compare what you receive against them:
  tool source commit:        <to be fixed by the owner before the ceremony>
  circuit "normal"   R1CS:   <digest from ceremony.json; published in advance>
  circuit "boundary" R1CS:   <digest from ceremony.json; published in advance>
  previous contribution:     <hash the participant before you posted publicly>
If any of these do not match what the coordinator sends you, stop and say so publicly. Do not contribute.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. STEPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1 — get the code and build the tool (one time, about 5 minutes)
    git clone <repository URL>            # the public XMR20 research repository
    cd <repository>/ceremony
    git checkout <tool source commit>     # the commit published in section 3
    cargo build --release
    # the tool is now at target/release/xmr20-params-ceremony

Step 2 — receive the ceremony folder from the coordinator
    You get a folder (we will call it CEREMONY) containing ceremony.json and the contribution files so far.
    Check that the hash of the latest contribution in ceremony.json equals the hash the previous participant posted
    publicly. If you cannot find their post, ask them, not us.

Step 3 — make your contribution (one command per circuit)
    Type a long random string as your entropy: dice rolls, a sentence nobody could guess, keyboard mashing. The tool
    mixes your text with your operating system's random generator, so the text does not have to be perfect.

    ./target/release/xmr20-params-ceremony contribute CEREMONY normal   phase2 --participant <your-handle> --entropy "<your random text>"
    ./target/release/xmr20-params-ceremony contribute CEREMONY boundary phase2 --participant <your-handle> --entropy "<different random text>"

    Each command prints one line of JSON ending in a "hash" value and writes one new file:
      CEREMONY/normal/phase2/NNN-<your-handle>.bin     (about 35 MB)
      CEREMONY/boundary/phase2/NNN-<your-handle>.bin   (about 22 MB)
    It also appends your entry to CEREMONY/ceremony.json.

Step 4 — publish your two hashes somewhere we cannot edit
    Post the two "hash" values from Step 3 on your own channel, with the date and the words "XMR20 key ceremony".
    This is what lets anyone later check that your step is really in the final transcript and was not replaced.

Step 5 — send the folder to the coordinator
    Send CEREMONY (or just the two new .bin files plus ceremony.json) back to the coordinator. Any channel is fine;
    the files are public. The next participant builds on your result.

Step 6 — clean up
    Reboot your computer (this clears the memory the secret lived in), then delete the CEREMONY folder and the
    terminal history line that contains your entropy text. Keep your two hashes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. AFTER THE CEREMONY — HOW YOU CHECK WE DID NOT CHEAT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When all participants are done, the coordinator publishes the full folder (every contribution), the final parameter
files and attestation.json. From a fresh checkout of the same tool commit, anyone can run:

    ./target/release/xmr20-params-ceremony verify <published CEREMONY folder> <published params folder>

This rebuilds the circuits from source, checks their fingerprints, replays every contribution (each must be valid
AND link to the one before it), recomputes the keys and compares them byte for byte with the published files and the
manifest that wallets ship. Look for your two hashes in attestation.json. If they are there and verify passes, your
honest deletion in Step 6 is enough to make the keys safe, regardless of what anyone else did.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT YOU ARE NOT BEING ASKED TO DO
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - You do not hold or send any funds, tokens or private keys.
  - You do not run a server or node, and nothing keeps running afterwards.
  - You do not need to understand the cryptography. The tool checks everything that can be checked.
  - You are not asked to vouch for XMR20. You are only asked to add randomness and then delete it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHO COUNTS AS A PARTICIPANT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A person who is not the XMR20 operator, running on hardware the operator does not control. Several machines, virtual
machines, accounts or AI processes belonging to the same person count as one participant. The ceremony needs people
who are independent of the operator AND of each other.
